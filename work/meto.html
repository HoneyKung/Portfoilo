METO — small interactive robot

A small robot that shows emotion, built to ask whether that helps beginners learn block coding — with a web app where learners program it.

Year
2025 – present (research with my advisor from Year 3, now my thesis)
Team / role
3 people — team lead
Tools
ESP32 · Tinkercad · 3D printing · Unity (digital twin) · web app
Status
in progress

Problem

The research question: does a small robot that shows emotion help beginners learn block-based programming, compared with one that doesn’t? The robot has to read as a tiny human-like companion, and the web side has to let anyone program it. The study is planned with first-year KMUTT students; the ethics (IRB) documents are still being prepared.

What I decided

  • Arms that clamp and lift, not a claw. A claw breaks the idea of a small human-like robot. Two joints at the shoulder, and a trapezoid body instead of a box so the arms can close in far enough to grip.
  • Make lifting actually work. The foam-board version could grip but not lift. First fix: rubber on the hand corners. Then printed concave oval arms that take many shapes, with small diamond-shaped studs so objects don’t slip — and a laser sensor under the body to check whether something was really lifted.
  • Wheels by testing, not by guess. Car wheels on the first prototype → printed tracks (two wheels: the belt slipped off, so I added a middle wheel) → tracks turned poorly, so the next version moves to two wheels plus a ball caster (not yet in CAD).
  • As small as possible. No heat-set inserts, to keep the robot small. The body is five layers that stack like a tiffin carrier, so it’s easy to assemble yourself and to modify.
  • A face that reacts. I designed and animated the screen expressions myself (9 of them). The camera reads the learner’s face to recognise their emotion; the robot turns to follow them and can mirror the learner’s expression on its screen face. It also shows emotion when code runs successfully with no error.
  • A web app for anyone. One account type for everyone — teacher or student roles come from joining a class. A skill counts only when code runs without error. No game words like XP or Level on the teacher side. Teachers can set their own tasks, and the AI helper gives hints, not answers. My advisor suggested the missions and the skill map; I worked out the details and drew the 62-piece robot avatar.

Result

A working robot with two arms, a screen face, an ESP32, two 18650 cells with BMS and USB-C charging; I wired the power myself (fuse, common ground) and it drives wirelessly. The printed oval arms grip and lift a cup. The “METO Robot” web runs on a local server with demo data — no public link yet. The user study has not started: IRB documents are in preparation.
